Ishara Venn was a Tollish planar geographer associated with the [University of Tollen](<../../gazetteer/greater-sembara/tollen/places/university-of-tollen.md>), active in the DR 1500s and early DR 1600s, after the [Great War](<../../events/1500s/great-war.md>). She is best known for [The Seams Between Worlds](<../../things/books/the-seams-between-worlds.md>), a systematic study of [elemental vortexes](<../../cosmology/planar-concepts/elemental-vortex.md>) and other planar connections.

Ishara's work treats elemental vortexes as temporary points of contact created when a nearly pure expression of an elemental energy draws an Energy Realm close to the [Material Plane](<../../cosmology/material-plane.md>). While many modern scholars treat her theories as controversial or fringe, [Emyr of Tafolwern](<emyr-of-tafolwern.md>) used her account of elemental vortexes as the starting point for his broader theory of planar upwellings.
